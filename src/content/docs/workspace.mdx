---
title: The workspace
description: The operator's app — signing in, tenants, facts, briefs, assets and the lead inbox, screen by screen.
---

The workspace is the app an operator drives Intent Studio with: a single-page app the server itself
serves at `/workspace` on the API's own origin. Same origin means no CORS and no tokens in browser
storage — the session cookie does everything. Every screen below is a call on
[the studio API](/api/reference/); nothing in the app can do something the API can't.

Open it at your server's address — `https://studio.example.com/workspace` in these examples.

## Signing in

The sign-in screen takes the operator password — the one whose argon2 hash is in
`INTENT_OPERATOR_PASSWORD_HASH`. A wrong password is a refusal, and the sign-in endpoint is rate
limited per address, so guesses cost time. A successful sign-in sets the session cookie and lands
on the tenant list; the session ends itself after its lifetime or too long idle, and a `401` from
any call drops the app back to the sign-in screen rather than pretending the call happened.

## Tenants

The home list is the tenants — one per client. Pick one, or create one with a name. Everything after
is inside that tenant: its facts, briefs, assets, sites and leads.

## Facts

The facts list shows every fact with its kind, statement and status — `proposed`, `approved`,
`stale`, `withdrawn` — plus its review date where one applies. Creating a fact picks a kind and asks
for the statement, the source, and an optional link; approving asks who approved it and when (with
an optional earlier review-by); withdrawing needs no body. A stale fact can't be re-approved without
a new approval date — the app makes that an action, not an error.

## Briefs

The brief editor is the site-in-waiting: business name, goal, intent and audience, services, brand
tone and colours, domains, references, and the claims — which of this tenant's approved facts a page
may assert. Saving a revision carries the revision it was read from; if someone else saved first the
app surfaces the conflict with the newer revision number rather than overwriting silently.

## Assets

The asset list is a tenant's uploaded files — images, the logo — each recorded with its kind,
content hash and size. Uploads are sent as raw bytes with the `x-intent-upload` marker (the CSRF
guard for non-form posts); the server stores them content-addressed and the app shows the metadata
PostgreSQL holds. A reference page can be fetched as a snapshot through the egress guard — it
refuses private addresses and caps size and time, and the refusal is the answer, not a retry.

## The inbox

`GET /v1/operator/leads` rendered: the newest lead first, paged by `before`, filterable by site.
Each lead shows what the submitter gave — name, email, phone, message, consent — plus its
acknowledgment status. A lead only exists because it and its outbox row committed together, so an
entry in the inbox means the notification was queued, not just received.

## What the app never does

- It never writes free text to a page — page content is the page tree the API validates.
- It never bypasses a refusal: a `422` with problems is shown as problems, by field.
- On a write that didn't answer (a dropped connection, a `503`), it says **it cannot tell whether
  the change was saved** — never "saved", never "failed". Repeating a safe read answers the truth.
