Three endpoints tell you the truth about a running server. On this machine's pages they show the
default address, `http://127.0.0.1:59400`.

## Liveness and readiness

| Endpoint | Answers | Caller |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /healthz` | The process is up and its event loop answers. | Anything — load balancers, uptime checks. |
| `GET /readyz` | The server is **ready to work**: the database answers and the store is usable. Behind Caddy, this is what a probe should hit — a `503` means the process lives but can't serve. | Orchestrators, deploy scripts, you. |
| `GET /metrics` | The counters and gauges — intake, outbox, sessions, limits — in Prometheus exposition. | Scrapers only, and only with `Authorization: Bearer $INTENT_METRICS_TOKEN` when a token is configured. |

`/readyz` failing while `/healthz` answers is the database-down or store-broken shape: the process
is honest about it rather than accepting requests it can't complete.

## What to alert on

- **`/readyz` down** — the service isn't serving; page-worthy.
- **Outbox depth growing** — lead notifications are accumulating (a notifier outage or a retry
  storm); `metrics` carries it.
- **Dead outbox rows** — attempts exhausted; the notification needs an operator, not more retries.
- **`429`/`503` rates on intake** — either abuse finding the limit or a capacity line you should
  raise deliberately.
- **Session and limit-table growth** — the bounded tables are cleaned on a schedule, and `metrics`
  shows whether they keep pace.

## Logs

`INTENT_LOG_FORMAT=json` (the default) gives one structured event per line for your collector;
`INTENT_LOG` filters (`info,sqlx=warn` by default). The server's own rules apply to what can appear
in a log line: no secrets, no password material, no lead PII, no brief internals — a log leak is a
bug with a test, not a style choice. If you ever see one, it's a defect to report.

## Proving it

The failure drills in the product repo exercise db-down, kill -9, notify-down and limit behaviour
against the real stack; a green `/readyz` after restart is the operator-facing proof of the same
contract.
