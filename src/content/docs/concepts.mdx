Intent Studio's model is small and every part of it answers a question a page must be able to answer.
These examples show `https://studio.example.com`, a reserved example domain — your server answers the
same calls at its own address.

## Tenant

One client. All of a client's facts, briefs, assets, sites and leads hang off their tenant, and no
route ever crosses tenants: `{tenant}` in a path is checked for shape, then for existence.

```http not-run="shown against the reserved example origin; the quick start runs the same call"
POST /v1/operator/tenants
{"name": "acme-plumbing"}
```

## Fact

A claim the business stands behind, with its source and its kind: testimonial, metric, price,
guarantee, licence, certification, insurance, service area, opening hours, contact details, or a
legal promise. A fact has a lifecycle the API enforces:

- `proposed` — recorded, not usable. Nothing may cite it.
- `approved` — a person approved it (`approved_by`, `approved_on`); it also gets a `review_by` day.
- `stale` — the review day passed. Stale is terminal for citing: the fact must be re-approved or
  withdrawn.
- `withdrawn` — retired deliberately.

The lifecycle is the honesty mechanism: a page can only assert what an approved, current fact
supports, and the server — not the author — says which facts qualify today.

## Brief

What the client's site is for, and the budget of truth it may spend. A brief carries the business
name, its goal (today, `quote_requests`: qualified quote requests through the site's form), the
intent and audience in the client's words, the services offered, the brand notes, the domains the
site may link to, the references to build from — and `claims`, the fact ids pages of this site may
use. Briefs are revisioned: a `PUT` must name the `expected_revision` it builds on, and a stale
expectation is a `409`, not a lost update.

## Page tree and spec-check

A page is never free HTML. It is a **page tree**: components of the `quote-request@0` vocabulary —
hero, services, testimonials, trust items, service area, hours, contact, the quote form, text, the
footer — each with typed slots. Text may be plain (no claim allowed) or bound to fact ids that
support it.

`POST …/briefs/{brief}/spec-check` validates a tree without storing anything: every claim slot
references an approved, current fact the brief lists; every link is structural (an in-page anchor,
a page of the tree, a contact fact's link, or an `https` address on one of the brief's domains);
free text passes a claim lint; a home page carries the quote form offering only the brief's
services. Problems come back by path, all of them at once — never one at a time.

## Asset and the content store

Uploaded files (photos, the logo) and fetched reference pages live outside PostgreSQL in a
content-addressed store: the bytes' SHA-256 is their name, so what you read back is provably what
was written. PostgreSQL records the asset's id, kind, content hash and provenance; the store only
holds bytes. A reference fetch is never a raw `curl` — it goes through an egress guard that refuses
private addresses and caps size and time.

## Lead and outbox

A published site's contact form posts to `POST /v1/sites/{site}/leads` — the only public write, and
the only route that needs no session. The lead and its notification outbox row **commit in the same
transaction**: the acknowledgment (`status: "stored"`) means both are durably recorded. A notifier
that is down loses nothing — the outbox retries on a schedule — and a resubmission with the same
`submission_id` returns the stored lead rather than a second copy.

## The operator session

One operator, one password (its argon2 hash is configured, never the password). Sign-in returns an
`HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict` cookie; every workspace call carries it. The workspace app is served
by the same origin, so there is no CORS and no token in storage.

## PostgreSQL is the authority

Every record — tenants, facts, briefs, revisions, sessions, assets' metadata, sites, leads, the
outbox, the audit trail — lives in PostgreSQL. Nothing else is authoritative: not the content
store (bytes only), not memory, not a file. If it isn't committed, it didn't happen.
